    Measure

    Height and weight for each learner. The result appears straight away.

    How to measure properly — read this first

    Before you start

    • Put the weighing scale on a hard, flat floor. Never on carpet, soil or a tilted floor.
    • Set the scale to zero. Check it with something you know the weight of — a sealed 1-litre bottle of water is 1.0 kg. Do this three times and make sure you get the same reading.
    • Fix the height board or tape flat against a wall with no baseboard. Check that it is straight up and down, and that 0 cm sits exactly on the floor.

    Weight

    • Light clothes. No shoes, no jacket, empty pockets, no bag or ID lace.
    • The learner stands still in the middle of the scale, arms at the sides, looking straight ahead.
    • Read to the nearest 0.1 kg once the number stops changing.

    Height

    • No shoes, no cap. Undo buns and thick braids on top of the head.
    • Heels together. Back of the head, shoulders, buttocks and heels touching the board where the body allows.
    • Head level — imagine a straight line from the bottom of the eye to the ear hole; it should be flat, not tilted up or down.
    • Ask the learner to breathe in and stand tall. Bring the headpiece down onto the hair. Read at your eye level to the nearest 0.1 cm.

    Checking your work

    • Measure every 10th learner a second time without looking at the first result. If height differs by more than 0.5 cm or weight by more than 0.1 kg, measure that whole batch again.
    • SIBOL warns you about impossible numbers, but it cannot tell that a scale is always 2 kg wrong. Only checking the scale can.
