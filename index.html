Anthropometric protocol & calibration checklist — read before you measure

Before the session

  • Place the scale on a hard, flat, level surface. Never on carpet, soil or a tilted floor.
  • Zero the scale. Verify it with a known weight (a sealed 1 L bottle of water = 1.0 kg); repeat three times and confirm the same reading.
  • Fix the height board or tape to a wall with no skirting board. Check with a spirit level or plumb line that it is vertical, and that 0 cm sits exactly on the floor.
  • Record the room and the equipment used, so a repeat round can use the same setup.

Weight

  • Light clothing, no shoes, no jacket, pockets emptied, no bag or ID lace.
  • Learner stands still in the centre of the platform, arms at sides, looking straight ahead.
  • Read to the nearest 0.1 kg once the display stops changing.

Height (standing, 5 years and older)

  • No shoes, no headwear; undo buns and thick braids on the crown.
  • Heels together, back of head, shoulder blades, buttocks, calves and heels touching the board where the body shape allows.
  • Head in the Frankfort plane: the line from the lower edge of the eye socket to the ear canal opening is horizontal.
  • Ask the learner to breathe in and stand tall; lower the headpiece to compress the hair; read at eye level to the nearest 0.1 cm.

Quality control

  • Re-measure every 10th learner independently. If height differs by more than 0.5 cm or weight by more than 0.1 kg, re-measure the whole batch.
  • SIBOL flags biologically implausible values, but it cannot detect a scale that is consistently 2 kg out. Only calibration can.

WHO reference data

Why this is a separate step

SIBOL classifies learners against the WHO Growth Reference 5–19 years (de Onis et al., 2007) using the LMS parameters WHO publishes. Those parameters are numbers; getting one wrong misclassifies real children. They are therefore never typed in by hand or reproduced from memory — they are converted directly from the official WHO spreadsheets.

How to load them

  1. Download the four expanded tables for constructing national health cards (z-scores, boys and girls) for BMI-for-age and Height-for-age from who.int — links are in the project README.
  2. Run python3 build_who_lms.py in the project folder. It reads the four .xlsx files and writes who-lms.json.
  3. Deploy who-lms.json beside index.html. SIBOL loads it automatically and caches it offline.

Method

For a measurement X at a given sex and age in completed months, with WHO parameters L, M, S:

z = ((X / M)L − 1) / (L · S)   (and z = ln(X / M) / S when L = 0)

For BMI-for-age, WHO restricts the Box–Cox distribution to the interval where empirical data exist. Beyond ±3 SD, SIBOL applies WHO's documented linear extrapolation rather than the raw LMS value:

z > 3 → 3 + (X − SD3⁺) / (SD3⁺ − SD2⁺)
z < −3 → −3 + (X − SD3⁻) / (SD2⁻ − SD3⁻)

Height-for-age is a length-based indicator and is not truncated. Cut-offs follow WHO and DepEd School Form 8:

About SIBOL

SIBOL — Filipino, to sprout, to grow. DepEd already measures every learner twice a year for School Form 8. Those measurements decide who receives School-Based Feeding Program support, and then they stop being useful: recorded on paper, hand-classified against WHO tables, submitted upward as compliance counts. The teacher who took the measurement rarely gets an interpretation back, and the nutrition data is never joined to the learning data sitting in the same class record.

SIBOL joins them, offline, on the phone the teacher already owns, and answers the question the system currently cannot: are the learners who are wasted or stunted the same learners who are failing to read?

Honest limitations

  • SIBOL detects association, not causation. A significant chi-square between nutritional status and learning band does not mean malnutrition caused the reading difficulty. It means the two are occurring in the same children, which is enough to justify acting.
  • Output quality is capped by measurement quality. An uncalibrated scale or a badly-positioned head corrupts every z-score downstream. The protocol guide is a core feature, not an extra.
  • SIBOL does not diagnose. It flags learners for teacher and health-worker attention. Severe cases go to a health professional, always.
  • The learning indicator is teacher-reported and coarse. Adequate for triage and priority-setting; not a validated literacy instrument.
  • Child health data carries real obligations. Local-first storage and anonymisation mode are deliberate design responses, but a pilot still needs school consent and a data agreement.

Reference

de Onis M, Onyango AW, Borghi E, Siyam A, Nishida C, Siekmann J. Development of a WHO growth reference for school-aged children and adolescents. Bulletin of the World Health Organization 2007;85(9):660–667.
